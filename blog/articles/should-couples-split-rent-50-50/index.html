Should Couples Split Rent 50/50? A Fairer Way to Share the Bills

Splitting everything down the middle feels fair. When one of you earns more, it quietly isn't. Here is the simple math couples use instead.

Paycheck Smarter · 6 min read · October 29, 2026

For couples who split everything 50/50 and still end the month with one person broke and the other one fine: the split is equal, but it is not fair.

If you take home $4,000 and your partner takes home $2,000, a $1,800 rent split in half costs you 22.5% of your pay and costs them 45% of theirs. Same rent. Very different month.

Is splitting rent 50/50 fair?

It is fair when you earn about the same. When incomes are different, 50/50 means the lower earner pays a much bigger slice of their life for the same apartment. That is usually where the quiet resentment starts, and it is rarely about the money itself.

The proportional split, step by step

  1. 01Add both monthly take-home paychecks.
  2. 02Divide each person's pay by the total. That is their share.
  3. 03Add up the shared bills: rent, utilities, internet, groceries.
  4. 04Each person pays their share of that total.

An example couple

Nate takes home $4,000 a month. Maya takes home $2,000. Together: $6,000.

  • Nate's share: 4,000 / 6,000 = two thirds (about 67%)
  • Maya's share: 2,000 / 6,000 = one third (about 33%)

What to keep separate

Shared bills go through the split. Personal spending stays personal: your own phone, your own debt, your own coffee. Many couples use one joint account for the shared bills and keep their own accounts for the rest. Each person sends their share to the joint account on payday.

Talk about it without a fight

Bring numbers, not complaints. "Here is what 50/50 leaves each of us after rent" lands better than "this isn't fair". Do the math together, once, on a calm evening, not the day a bill bounces.
